
## **1st Industrial Revolution (mid-1700s to early 1800s)**

🔹 **Steam engine (improved)** – *James Watt* improved the steam engine and patented his design in **1769**, transforming machine power and industry. ([Encyclopedia Britannica][1])
🔹 **Spinning Jenny (textile machine)** – *James Hargreaves* invented it around **1764–1765** to speed up yarn production. ([Wikipedia][2])
🔹 **Water frame** – *Richard Arkwright* adapted a spinning machine to use water power and helped create modern factories (late **1760s–1770s**). ([Wikipedia][3])
🔹 **Spinning mule** – *Samuel Crompton* created this in **1779**, improving thread quality and productivity. ([Encyclopedia Britannica][1])
🔹 **Power loom** – *Edmund Cartwright* patented the power loom in **1785** for weaving cloth faster. ([Encyclopedia Britannica][1])
🔹 **Steam locomotive (railway engine)** – *Richard Trevithick* built the first steam locomotive in **1803** and *George Stephenson*’s “Rocket” ran in **1830**. ([Encyclopedia Britannica][4])
🔹 **Cotton gin** – *Eli Whitney* patented it in **1793** to separate cotton seeds quickly (important but overlapping). ([Encyclopedia Britannica][1])

**Main idea:** Mechanisation of production with steam power and early factories. ([Encyclopedia Britannica][5])

---

## **2nd Industrial Revolution (mid-1800s to early 1900s)**

🔹 **Electric incandescent light** – *Joseph Swan* and *Thomas Edison* independently developed practical electric light bulbs by **1878–1880**. ([Storia][6])
🔹 **Telephone** – *Alexander Graham Bell* patented the telephone in **1876**. ([Storia][6])
🔹 **Air brake for trains** – *George Westinghouse* patented it in **1872** to make rail travel safer. ([Storia][6])
🔹 **Internal-combustion engine developments** – *Nikolaus Otto* developed the four-stroke engine in **1876**, *Gottlieb Daimler* and *Karl Benz* developed gasoline engines and early automobiles in the **1880s** (Britain/Germany contributions noted in sources). ([Encyclopedia Britannica][4])
🔹 **Steel production process (Bessemer)** – *Henry Bessemer* introduced the steel-making process in **1856**, revolutionizing construction and machinery. ([Wikipedia][7])
🔹 **Assembly line (mass production)** – *Henry Ford* perfected assembly-line car production in **1913**, massively increasing output. ([Encyclopedia Britannica][1])

**Main idea:** Electricity, steel, mass production, and communication revolutionised industry and everyday life. ([Storia][6])

---

## **3rd Industrial Revolution (mid-20th century onward)**

🔹 **Transistor** – inventors *John Bardeen, Walter Brattain, William Shockley* developed the first working transistor in **1947**, which was patented in the early 1950s, launching modern electronics. ([Wikipedia][8])
🔹 **Integrated circuit** – *Jack Kilby* and *Robert Noyce* created the IC in **1958–1959**, allowing multiple electronic components on one chip. ([Wikipedia][8])
🔹 **Microprocessor** – *Intel* released the first commercially available microprocessor (Intel 4004) in **1971**. ([Scribd][9])
🔹 **Personal computers and Internet foundations** – development continued through the **1970s–1990s** with mainframes, PCs, and networking, evolving from ARPANET to the early Internet. ([Wikipedia][8])

**Main idea:** Digital electronics, computers, and networking transformed production, communication, and society. ([Telefónica][10])

---

## **4th Industrial Revolution / Industry 4.0 (21st century – today)**

Unlike older revolutions, the 4th is *not defined by a few single dates or inventors* but by a **combination of modern technologies** that are reshaping industry and society:

🔹 **Internet of Things (IoT)** – network of connected sensors and devices exchanging data in real time. ([mckinsey.com][11])
🔹 **Cyber-physical systems & smart factories** – machines and computers integrated to automate and optimise production. ([Wikipedia][12])
🔹 **3D printing (additive manufacturing)** and **robotics** – enabling flexible, customized production. ([mckinsey.com][11])
🔹 **Artificial Intelligence (AI), Big Data, cloud computing** – providing advanced decision-making and automation. ([mckinsey.com][11])

🕐 **When it began:** The term “Industry 4.0” was first used in **2011** by German strategists and became widely recognised in industry literature around the mid-2010s. ([mckinsey.com][11])

**Main idea:** Hybrid integration of digital, physical, and biological systems and smart automation. ([Salesforce][13])

### **What is laser eye surgery used for?**

Laser eye surgery is mainly used to **correct vision problems** such as short-sightedness (myopia), long-sightedness (hyperopia), and astigmatism by reshaping the cornea. ([nhs.uk][7])

### **Can children be subject to laser eye surgery?**

No — laser eye surgery is usually **not suitable for children or young people** because their vision may still be changing. ([nhs.uk][8])

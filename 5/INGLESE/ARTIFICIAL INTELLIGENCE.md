### Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Artificial intelligence is the ability of computers or machines to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, and problem-solving.  
The goal of AI is to develop systems that can imitate human intellectual processes.

At present, AI systems cannot perform all the tasks that humans can do. They are very good at **specific and complex tasks** (for example playing chess, medical diagnosis, or voice recognition), but they **lack flexibility and everyday knowledge**.

There are two main methods of AI:

- **Symbolic (top-down) approach**: based on processing symbols and logical rules, without copying the biological brain.
    
- **Connectionist (bottom-up) approach**: based on artificial neural networks that imitate the structure of the human brain.

### Augmented Reality (AR)

Augmented reality integrates **digital information with the real world in real time**.  
Unlike virtual reality, AR does not create a completely artificial environment, but **adds digital elements to the real environment**.

The main advantage of AR is that it combines digital content with real-world perception. It is used for **decision-making and entertainment** and can be accessed through devices like **smartphones, tablets, and smart glasses**.

AR requires specific hardware components:

- a processor
    
- sensors
    
- a display
    
- input devices

- **With reference to the tasks they can perform, what is the difference between a human being and an AI device?**  
    A human being can perform a wide variety of tasks with flexibility and everyday knowledge, while an AI device can only perform very specific and complex tasks and cannot match human flexibility.

    A computer can carry out specific and complex tasks such as discovering mathematical proofs, playing chess, medical diagnosis, running search engines, and voice or handwriting recognition.
    
- **Which method of artificial intelligence is more "practical"? Why?**  
    The symbolic (top-down) approach is more practical because it analyses cognitive processes independently of the biological brain and focuses on processing symbols, which is easier to implement in computer systems.
    
- **What type of world is experienced with AR?**  
    With augmented reality, users experience the real world enhanced with additional digital information in real time.
    
- **What hardware components are needed in AR?**  
    Augmented reality requires hardware components such as a processor, sensors, a display, and input devices.
